Feature Story

Double Extortion: The Ransomware Tactic Targeting Businesses Like Yours

Ransomware has evolved — and the old playbook of "just restore from backup" no longer tells the whole story. In double extortion attacks, criminals steal your data before encrypting it, then threaten to publish it publicly if the ransom isn't paid. That means even a clean backup doesn't eliminate the risk of exposure. Average ransom demands for small and mid-sized businesses now exceed $120,000, and attackers are using automation to target businesses of every size at scale. Effective defense requires layers: multi-factor authentication on every account, immutable backups that can't be altered or encrypted by attackers, endpoint detection, and a tested incident response plan. Knowing exactly who to call and what to do in the first hour of an attack can be the difference between a contained incident and a business-ending event. If you're unsure whether your defenses are ready, that's the conversation to have with us.

Cybersecurity

AI-Powered Phishing: Why Your Inbox Is More Dangerous Than Ever

Phishing emails in 2026 are no longer the obvious, typo-riddled scams of years past. AI-generated messages are now grammatically flawless, hyper-personalized, and crafted to mimic the exact tone of your vendors, bank, or colleagues. Common red flags — look-alike domains, urgent payment requests, unexpected attachments — remain, but spotting them takes active habit, not just awareness. Annual training videos aren't enough; research shows employees forget most of what they learn within weeks. Monthly phishing simulations paired with short micro-learning modules are significantly more effective at building lasting instincts. The goal isn't to catch people — it's to build a culture where pausing on a suspicious message is the norm, not the exception.

Data Protection

The 3-2-1-1 Backup Rule: Is Your Data Actually Protected?

The backup standard has evolved from the familiar 3-2-1 rule to a more resilient 3-2-1-1: three copies of your data, on two different media types, with one stored offsite — and one copy immutable. An immutable backup cannot be altered, deleted, or encrypted by anyone, including ransomware. That final "1" is what makes the difference when an attacker has already reached your network. Just as important as having backups is testing them. Most businesses only discover a broken backup when they urgently need it. A recovery drill — actually restoring a few files and timing the process — reveals gaps that checklists miss. If your last restore test has no date attached, it's overdue.

Tip of the Month — Summer Hardware

Don't Let the Heat Fry Your Equipment

Summer heat is one of the most overlooked causes of hardware failure and unexpected downtime. Servers, switches, and networking gear are designed to operate within a specific temperature range — typically 64°F to 80°F — and sustained heat above that shortens component lifespan and increases the chance of sudden failure. Now is the right time to check that any equipment closets or server rooms have adequate cooling and airflow, confirm that AC units serving those spaces are due for maintenance, and verify that UPS batteries are in good health before peak summer temperatures arrive. If your gear isn't monitored with temperature alerts, that's a quick win worth setting up.
